GC Reclamation Control Flow

Snapshots, Reader Protection, and Block Reuse with Correctness Proof

Part 1: Snapshot Registration & GC-Safe Control Flow
The Protective Envelope: At every moment during registration, GC sees at least one protection mechanism. Steps 1–3 overlap in a carefully choreographed sequence: bound publishes first (catches mid-registration readers), live_tx publishes next (catches newly-drawing readers), then bound clears (hands off protection). The order is critical and enforced by Release/Acquire semantics.
Why Ordering Matters: If live_tx were published BEFORE the bound, a concurrent GC scan could read live_tx before the write, then read in_flight_bound after it cleared—missing the reader entirely. But because the reader publishes the bound first, GC scanning in_flight_bound first ALWAYS sees mid-registration readers. This is the fundamental guarantee that prevents races.
Part 2: Dead Blocks Queue Structure
Why ordering matters: Blocks are ordered by death version, so the GC can drain from the front without scanning the entire queue. Once a block fails the eligibility check, all remaining blocks in that queue are also ineligible.
Part 3: Block Reclamation Decision Logic
Critical Safety Check: A block is only eligible if its death version is strictly less than every active or in-flight snapshot. This ensures no reader—current or mid-registration—can ever access that block.
Part 4: Correctness & Race-Freedom Proof

Why Block Reclamation is Safe During Reader Registration

The system guarantees that no block a reader needs can be reclaimed before or during its snapshot registration. This is proven by the careful ordering of in_flight_bound and live_tx publication.

1 Reader draws ts_start at version V1
The global clock's next_timestamp() returns V1. Any block with death_version < V1 was dead before the reader started.
2 in_flight_bound is published BEFORE ts_start is drawn
The bound = current_version() is read and stored in in_flight_bound[worker] BEFORE calling next_timestamp(). Since the clock only increases, bound ≤ ts_start always.
3 live_min_snapshot() reads in_flight_bound BEFORE live_tx
This ordering is critical. The reader publishes to live_tx AFTER publishing the bound, and clears the bound AFTER live_tx insert. GC scanning in_flight_bound first guarantees it sees the registration in progress.
4 GC cannot reclaim blocks the reader might need
Since in_flight_bound[worker] ≤ ts_start (from step 2), and GC only reclaims blocks with death_version < live_min_snapshot (which includes in_flight_bound), a block needed by the reader (death_version ≥ ts_start) is never reclaimed.
5 No gap between bounds and live_tx
The sequence is: publish bound → draw ts_start → publish live_tx → clear bound. Even if cleared prematurely (which doesn't happen), live_tx insert happened before clearing, so GC sees one mechanism or the other at any moment.

✓ Result: GC sees at least one of (in_flight_bound, live_tx) always protects the reader. No block the reader needs can be reclaimed.

The Key Insight: The system uses two overlapping protection mechanisms. During registration, neither disappears before the other is published. This creates a "protective envelope" around the reader that GC cannot penetrate.
